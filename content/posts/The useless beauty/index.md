### What is useless beauty ?

The useless beauty is the beauty that surrounds us every day because someone once believed it was necessary.

#### The old Street Lamps

![[StreetLamp.jpg|383]]


We have all seen those old street lamps: not particularly bright, nor particularly tall.  
Yet they were made with exquisite taste and radiated a warm yellow-orange light.  
They have since been replaced by far more practical street lamps.  
Better lighting, taller poles, a cooler color temperature—they make people feel safer and more alert, making them objectively better as public lighting.  
Here's the catch: they're also extremely ugly—just long metal poles covered in stickers and graffiti.  

" *Why would it matter ?* " some people would ask, and it's a great question.  
Why should we bother designing them beautifully when we can build them faster and more cheaply?

Now let's look at the other side of this Roman aqueduct.

![[rambarde-peyrou-montpellier-960x640.jpg]]

One thing should immediately catch your eye: the guardrail.
Like the street lamps earlier, it does its job perfectly. It's cheap, easy to replace, and neutral.  
Yet, it could have been beautiful.  

#### Why such a big deal ?

In the past, people seemed to care about creating beautiful public spaces when designing their cities. My guess is that it was a display of the city's wealth. Yet cities today are wealthier than they have ever been. 

If money is the only issue,  
"Why should you pay twice as much for a guardrail?"  
Because I believe the extra cost pays for beauty and a shared heritage.  
It completely deprives us of public spaces—spaces paid for through local taxes and intended to be enjoyed by everyone.

#### The broken windows Theory

This is this a 1982 [theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broken_windows_theory) which, in short, argues that visible signs of neglect and disorder encourage further crime and antisocial behaviour.   

What's the link with beauty?  

By leaving broken windows unrepaired—and therefore depriving us of the beauty of a well-maintained building—we create a more hostile public environment that is more prone to antisocial behavior, and vandalism.

More importantly, it deprives us from something far more important than anything:

#### The third place

A third place is a place that is neither home (the first place) nor work (the second place), but a public, neutral, and welcoming environment where people can meet and build social connections without the specific purpose of working or consuming.  
the characteristics of a third place are rather simple, yet precise:
- Available to all, small  or no fee to enter
- Neutral, no one is at home there
- Make spontaneous interaction possible
- Lot of regular people yet open to new ones

Parks, town square, Cafe, Pub, Shopping Mall etc...  
You probably now understand where i am going with this.
Ornament, noble materials, gardens, park, public places etc.. are not luxury, it's an investment for collective life.

> *Beauty has a financial cost. Neglect has a social one.*  
#### One's home

Nowadays when you look at decoration photo, or Instagram or anything that show home arrangement we always see the same colors, same materials and (i'm not exempt of it) everything is Grey, Beige, White, Cheap Wood. Everywhere, everything.

When i think of it, it feels that we live in our own space like they do not belong to us—witch is mostly the case since people my age can't ~~f~~ afford a home.
We no longer inhabit a place; we leave it exactly as we found it—just enough to get our deposit back.

#### End note

We have become remarkably good at building efficient places. Perhaps it's time to build places worth inheriting again.
